# Stakeholder conversations

Three 15-minute conversations to test BUFFER's assumptions against people who live with or work on
the problem. Record what they say, not what we hoped they would say. Disclose these conversations as
outside help in the submission (competition rules, section 13.4).

Before each call: ask permission to take notes and, separately, to quote them by role (not name) in the
video and slides.

---

## 1. WASH researcher

**Who:** _(role and institution, e.g. lecturer, Institute of Water and Flood Management, BUET)_
**Date:**  **Quote permission:** yes / no

Questions (pick 4-5):
1. Our model assumes 4 L per person per day for drinking and cooking, and 6 L of flexible use taken from stored rainwater. Do those match what you have seen?
2. What tank sizes are typical in household rainwater systems in Khulna and Satkhira?
3. Is 30 mm of rain over 3 days a sensible definition of a "reliable recharge"?
4. Which non-drinking uses would you consider acceptable for pond or shallow-well water, and what testing would you require first?
5. What would make you doubt a result like "70 shortage days reduced to 0.5"?

## 3. Coastal household

**Who:** _(household role, e.g. the person who manages water; village and upazila only)_
**Date:**  **Quote permission:** yes / no

Questions (in Bangla, simple words):
1. This dry season, was there a time when you had no good drinking water? How long?
2. What did you use instead? Did you buy water? How much did it cost?
3. Which tasks do you already do with pond or other water?
4. If a small box told you "your drinking water will last 20 days, rain is 30 days away", would that help? What would you do differently?
5. Would you trust a machine to choose which water goes to the toilet tap? Why or why not?
