# 1. Executive snapshot

The **Xylem Global Student Innovation Challenge 2026** is an approximately eight-week, fully virtual global competition for students aged **13 to 25**. It is sponsored by **Xylem** and organized/hosted by **BeMyApp** as part of the broader **Xylem Ignite** youth program.

Participants can work alone or in teams of up to **five** students and must choose **one** of three challenge areas:

1. **Water Quantity** — meeting the water demands of a changing world.
2. **Water Quality** — safeguarding water resources from pollution and degradation.
3. **Water Access** — enabling reliable, equitable, affordable and resilient access to water, sanitation and hygiene services.

The 2026 competition has separate **High School / Secondary** and **University / Tertiary** tracks. The overall prize pool is **US$20,000**, split evenly between the two tracks.

The competition period is stated in the Official Rules as **28 September 2026 through 20 November 2026**, followed by judging from **23 November through 9 December 2026**. The winners are expected to be announced **18 December 2026**, tentatively.

The main project requirements include a completed project page, all team members registered on the platform, supporting presentation slides, disclosure of outside assistance, and a **3–4 minute video presentation/demo**.

Projects are judged on three criteria:

- **Impact**
- **Challenge Fit / Feasibility**
- **Innovation**

Each criterion is scored by judges on a **1–5 scale**, making **15 points maximum per submission** according to the Official Rules.

# 13. Submission requirements

The Official Rules define several minimum project submission requirements.

## 13.1 Project page

Create and complete a project page on the event platform.

The project page must include all required fields.

Project area:
https://xyleminnovationchallenge-platform.bemyapp.com/#/projects

## 13.2 Register the whole team

Every team member must:
- register individually
- then be added to the project page's **Our Team** section

## 13.3 Supporting presentation materials

Teams must create **presentation slides**.

The slides:
- may be used during the video presentation
- should be added to the project page
- may be provided through a public/viewable Google Slides link
- or uploaded as a PDF

If using externally hosted files, ensure the judges can access them.

## 13.4 Disclosure of outside help

The Official Rules say participants must mention outside assistance received, including examples such as:
- teachers
- professors
- outside mentors

This is important for transparency.

## 13.5 Video presentation / demo

Required:
- a **brief 3–4 minute video presentation/demo**

The rules say the video should cover:
- introduction of the team
- whether the team is in the high school or university category
- which challenge is being addressed
- what the project is
- a short demo of the solution

A link to the video must be shared on the platform project page.

If using Google Drive, it must be public/viewable for the judges.

## 13.6 English requirement

The Official Rules say:
- the submission must, at a minimum, **support English-language use**

This does not necessarily mean every artifact must be English-only, but English support is a minimum formal requirement.

## 13.7 Publishing is required

The participant platform states that completing a project page is not enough.

The team must click **“Publish”** on the project page for it to be officially submitted for judging.

An unpublished project page is not considered submitted.

## Critical source conflict about the exact time

There are multiple incompatible deadline-time statements across official sources.

### Official Rules
The Official Rules state:

**1:00 PM Eastern Time (ET), November 20, 2026**

### Official public landing page
The public challenge landing page states:

**1:00 PM PT / 4:00 PM ET / 8:00 PM UTC**

Those three times are not mutually consistent for 20 November 2026.  
On that date, 1:00 PM Pacific Standard Time corresponds to 4:00 PM Eastern Standard Time and 9:00 PM UTC, not 8:00 PM UTC.

### Registered participant platform
The platform states:

**1:00 PM PT / 4:00 PM ET / 9:00 PM UTC**

This set is internally consistent for standard time in November.

### Practical interpretation
The **legal Official Rules** and the **challenge platform/landing page** therefore disagree by several hours.

For risk management:
- do not plan to submit near the last possible hour
- submit significantly earlier on 20 November
- verify any platform countdown or organizer update
- contact the organizer if exact deadline timing is material

# 17. Judging criteria

The final project is evaluated in three areas.

## 17.1 Impact

Questions/considerations described by organizers include:
- How meaningful is the potential impact?
- What is the potential reach?
- Is there market opportunity/potential?
- How could communities, people or the environment benefit?
- What social impact could the solution create?

## 17.2 Challenge Fit & Feasibility

Questions/considerations include:
- How directly does the solution address the selected problem statement?
- How relevant is the approach?
- Does it effectively solve the stated problem?
- Could it realistically be implemented in a real-world environment?

## 17.3 Innovation

Questions/considerations include:
- Is the solution original?
- Is it forward-thinking?
- Is the concept new or disruptive?
- Does it take a fresh approach to an existing problem?
- Is the experience/solution compelling for its intended users?

# 18. Judging score mechanics

The Official Rules provide a scoring detail that is not prominent on the public landing page.

Each judge scores each eligible submission on:
- Impact
- Challenge Fit / Feasibility

Maximum:
**15 points per submission per judge**

The rules say:
- winners are selected based on scores received
- scores will not be made public
- decisions are final and cannot be challenged

# 40. Recommended LLM interpretation of “what does it take to win?”

This section is an analytical synthesis, not an additional official rule.

A strong 2026 submission should likely demonstrate all three official judging dimensions simultaneously:

## Impact
Show:
- a clearly defined user/community/industry problem
- who benefits
- scale/reach
- measurable outcomes
- market or deployment potential
- environmental/social value

## Challenge fit and feasibility
Show:
- direct connection to one selected problem statement
- evidence the problem is real
- implementation pathway
- technical plausibility
- operating constraints
- cost/resources
- deployment context
- risks and mitigations
- a working prototype/demo where possible

## Innovation
Show:
- what is genuinely new
- what existing approach it improves/replaces
- why the new approach matters
- a compelling user experience or implementation model
- defensible differentiation

Because the formal video is only **3–4 minutes**, the pitch needs to communicate:
**problem → solution → proof/demo → impact → feasibility → novelty** efficiently.

## Official one-page problem brief: key evidence/data

The official Water Quantity problem-statement brief supplied through the registered portal states that:

- roughly **4 billion people experience severe water scarcity for at least one month annually**
- about **one-quarter of the world's population** faces extremely high water stress, meaning it uses more than 80% of renewable freshwater resources
- **21 of the world's 37 largest aquifers** are being depleted faster than they recharge
- Google reported withdrawing about **5.6 billion gallons of water in 2022**, with about **5.2 billion gallons used by data centers**, representing an approximately 20% year-over-year increase
- research from the University of California, Riverside estimated that training **GPT-3** required approximately **700,000 liters of freshwater** during the training process in Microsoft data centers
- Xylem estimates industries supporting the AI economy, including data centers, semiconductor fabs and power generation, withdrew approximately **23.7 km³ of water in 2025**, around a 38% increase versus 2020
- Xylem projects water use across that AI-related value chain could grow by an additional **129% by 2050** if current trends continue
- the brief states that large hyperscale data centers can consume **up to 5 million gallons of water per day**, comparable to the daily water use of a town of roughly 10,000–50,000 residents

> In 2025, Xylem enabled the reuse of more than 22 billion cubic meters of water and reduced more than 4.3 billion cubic meters of non-revenue water.

"Non-revenue water" is described in the brief as water lost to leaks, meter inaccuracies or unauthorized use.

**Problem Statement #2: Water Quality Challenge — Safeguarding water resources from pollution and degradation**

## Portal summary

Water quality is essential to:

- human health
- economic development
- environmental sustainability

The portal asks teams to develop solutions that prevent, detect, monitor, remove or reduce pollutants that affect water quality and human health.

## Expanded challenge context

Threats identified on the challenge card include:

- urbanization
- industrialization
- agricultural runoff
- plastic pollution
- inadequately treated wastewater
- PFAS
- microplastics
- pharmaceuticals
- other emerging contaminants

The portal notes that water-quality solutions may use:

- science
- technology
- community action
- nature-based solutions
- policy innovation

## Official challenge ask

Develop innovative solutions that **prevent, detect, monitor, remove or reduce emerging contaminants or other pollutants that threaten water quality and human health**.

Projects should address present and emerging water-pollution problems through science, technology or other innovative approaches.

## Example solution areas listed by the portal

- Water-quality monitoring and detection
- PFAS detection, treatment or removal
- Microplastic detection and reduction
- Pharmaceutical and emerging-contaminant removal
- Wastewater-treatment solutions
- Agricultural-runoff reduction
- Industrial-pollution prevention
- Nature-based water-quality solutions
- Community water monitoring and action
- Pollution prevention and source reduction
- Sustainable water-management practices
- Policy and education solutions that protect water resources

## Official one-page problem brief: key evidence/data

The official Water Quality problem-statement brief states that:

- approximately **2.2 billion people lack safely managed drinking-water services**
- another recent WHO/UNICEF estimate cited on the page says about **2.1 billion people** still lack access to safely managed drinking water
- at least **1.7 billion people use a drinking-water source contaminated with feces**
- microbially contaminated drinking water contributes to approximately **505,000 diarrheal deaths annually**
- industry and agriculture remain major contributors to water pollution worldwide
- WHO has identified **18 priority PFAS compounds** for ongoing health-risk assessment
- PFAS, or **per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances**, are described as highly significant emerging water pollutants associated with cancer, immune-system effects, developmental impacts and other health concerns
- the brief says only **56% of domestic wastewater is safely treated globally**, limiting safe-reuse opportunities and increasing pressure on natural ecosystems
- a figure caption says that in **2024 the U.S. EPA established nationwide drinking-water standards for PFAS** and that the rule could help protect approximately **100 million people**

### Xylem-specific impact statement in the brief

The brief states:

> In 2025, Xylem prevented over 13 billion cubic meters of polluted water from entering communities and waterways.

## Water Quality solution hypothesis

The official brief asks participants to develop innovative solutions that:

- prevent contaminants
- detect contaminants
- monitor contaminants
- remove contaminants
- reduce contaminants

It explicitly lists:

- PFAS
- microplastics
- pharmaceuticals
- other pollutants affecting water quality and human health

The solution should also promote sustainable water management using one or more of:

- technology
- community action
- nature-based solutions
- policy innovation

## Water Quality problem-brief references

The one-page brief cites sources including:

1. Xylem, **2025 Sustainability Report**
2. World Health Organization, **Water Sanitation and Health** monitoring/evidence material
3. WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme for Water Supply, Sanitation and Hygiene
4. WHO drinking-water fact sheet
5. WHO diarrhoeal-disease fact sheet
6. UN-Water material on water scarcity
7. UN-Water, **Water Quality and Wastewater**
8. WHO material assessing the occurrence and human-health risk of PFAS

The portal-provided Water Quality PDF is a one-page visual challenge brief and should be retained as primary 2026 challenge evidence.

# 49. Detailed 2026 Challenge Card — Water Access

## Official problem statement title

**Problem Statement #3: Water Access Challenge — Enabling universal access to reliable, affordable WASH services**

WASH = **Water, Sanitation and Hygiene**.

## Portal summary

Access to safe, reliable and affordable water remains a challenge for billions of people worldwide.

The challenge asks teams to improve **reliable, equitable and resilient access to water** by addressing barriers that may be:

- physical
- economic
- environmental
- infrastructure-related

## Expanded challenge context

The challenge card explains that water access is fundamental to:

- public health
- education
- economic opportunity
- human dignity

Barriers highlighted include:

- water scarcity
- aging or inadequate infrastructure
- pollution
- affordability
- climate change
- growing demand
- rural/remote isolation
- service disruptions
- groundwater depletion

The challenge especially highlights rural, remote and underserved communities.

## Official challenge ask

Develop innovative solutions that improve **reliable, equitable and resilient access to water** by addressing physical, economic, environmental or infrastructure barriers.

Solutions can take forms such as:

- new technologies
- improvements to existing systems
- community-based approaches
- policy innovations
- sustainable water-management practices
- other creative approaches that expand and strengthen access to water and WASH services

## Example solution areas listed by the portal

- Affordable drinking-water access
- WASH infrastructure and services
- Rural and remote water systems
- Climate-resilient water solutions
- Solutions for underserved or water-insecure communities
- Decentralized water systems
- Water affordability
- Community-led water management
- Sanitation and hygiene solutions
- Infrastructure monitoring and maintenance
- Sustainable water-supply systems
- Solutions that reduce the time and burden of collecting water

## Official one-page problem brief: key evidence/data

The official Water Access problem-statement brief states that:

- an estimated **2.2 billion people** lack safely managed drinking-water services
- **3.5 billion people** lack safely managed sanitation
- **2 billion people** lack basic handwashing facilities with soap and water at home
- at least **1.7 billion people** continue to use drinking-water sources contaminated with feces
- the brief cites approximately **505,000 diarrheal deaths annually** from contaminated drinking water
- **106 million people** rely directly on untreated surface-water sources such as rivers, lakes and streams
- **1.8 billion people** live in households without water available on their premises and must collect water from external sources
- women and girls are responsible for collecting water in **7 out of 10 households** that do not have water on-site
- the visual caption states that women and girls spend **250 million hours every day collecting water worldwide**
- the brief emphasizes that climate change makes water availability less predictable and reliable for vulnerable communities
- access to safe drinking water and sanitation is recognized by the United Nations as a fundamental human right, yet billions remain underserved

### Xylem-specific 2030 commitment in the brief

The brief states:

> By 2030, Xylem aims to improve water security for 80 million people by enabling climate-resilient WASH access and capacity-building in under-resourced, water-insecure communities.

## Water Access solution hypothesis

The official brief asks teams to develop innovative solutions that improve:

- reliability
- equity
- resilience
- affordability
- sustainability

of water access and WASH services.

It explicitly suggests addressing physical, economic, environmental or infrastructure barriers through:

- technology
- community action
- policy innovation
- sustainable water-management practices

## Water Access problem-brief references

The one-page brief cites sources including:

1. Xylem, **2025 Sustainability Report**
2. WorldMetrics water-crisis statistics
3. UN-Water, **Progress on Wastewater Treatment: SDG 6 Indicator 6.3.1, 2024 Update**
4. UN-Water, **Indicator 6.3.1: Proportion of Domestic and Industrial Wastewater Flows Safely Treated**
5. UN-Water, **The United Nations World Water Development Report 2026: Water for All People: Equal Rights and Opportunities**
6. Food & Water Watch, **Tiny Particles, Big Problems: Microplastics in Our Drinking Water**
7. United Nations General Assembly resolution on the Human Right to Water and Sanitation and WHO/UNICEF JMP material

The portal-provided Water Access PDF is a one-page visual challenge brief and should be retained as primary 2026 challenge evidence.

# 50. Submission Requirements — Portal-confirmed version

The registered portal repeats the same core submission requirements for all three challenge tracks.

To be eligible for judging, teams must complete and **publish** their project page by the submission deadline.

Each submission must include:

## Project Page
Create a project page on the event platform and complete all required fields.

## Register Your Team
Add every team member to the **Our Team** section of the project page.

Every team member must first register individually before being added.

## Supporting Materials
Create presentation slides and add them to the project page.

Accepted portal guidance includes:
- Google Slides, with public/viewable access enabled
- uploaded PDF

Teams must disclose outside assistance from:
- teachers
- professors
- mentors
- other helpers

## Video Presentation / Demo
Create a **3–4 minute** video that includes:

1. team introduction
2. competition category: High School or University
3. selected challenge
4. explanation of the project
5. short demo of the solution

The video link must be added to the project page and must be publicly viewable.

## Publish requirement
The team must click **Publish**.

The portal explicitly warns that the project **will not be considered submitted until it is published**.

## Accessibility requirement
The portal's Project Presentation & Video Guide reminder states:

> The slide deck and video must both be publicly accessible. If judges cannot access the materials, the submission cannot be judged.

# 56. New analytical observations from the registered portal

These are analytical observations, not additional formal rules.

## 56.1 The 2026 Water Quantity challenge explicitly welcomes AI/data-center water projects

The detailed problem statement goes beyond conventional household water conservation. It specifically calls out:

- AI
- data centers
- cloud computing
- semiconductor manufacturing
- power generation

as growing sources of water demand.

That makes ideas involving:
- AI infrastructure water efficiency
- cooling optimization
- reuse/recycling in data centers
- water-aware compute scheduling
- leak detection
- industrial process optimization

directly aligned with the official challenge framing.

## 56.2 Water Quality is broad enough for technology, nature-based systems, policy and community action

A project does not have to be only a new filter or sensor. The official framing explicitly permits:

- technology
- community action
- nature-based solutions
- policy innovation
- education

provided the solution has a credible path to preventing, detecting, monitoring, removing or reducing water pollution.

## 56.3 Water Access is broader than “build a water pump”

The official challenge includes:
- affordability
- decentralized systems
- infrastructure monitoring
- maintenance
- community management
- sanitation/hygiene
- reducing water-collection burden
- climate resilience

This means software, logistics, finance, service-delivery and community-governance models can potentially fit if they improve reliable, equitable and resilient WASH access.

## 56.4 Past winners show that software-only or education-heavy projects can compete

The 2025 examples include:
- AI flood prediction
- offline communications
- educational platforms
- gamified learning
- interactive web applications
- SMS/mobile alert systems

This is evidence that a competitive project does not necessarily need to be a laboratory-grade hardware invention. The judging framework still requires meaningful impact, strong challenge fit/feasibility and innovation.

## 56.5 Presentation quality is operationally important

The platform has published a dedicated **Project Presentation & Video Guide**, and the portal repeatedly emphasizes:
- a clear slide deck
- a 3–4 minute video
- a short demo
- publicly accessible materials

This suggests the final judging experience depends heavily on how clearly a team packages and demonstrates its idea, not only on the underlying technical concept.
